## 🎛️ What You Can Do with Serum 2

Serum 2 gives you a flexible environment for building sounds from scratch. Instead of relying only on ready-made presets, you can create and shape your own sounds by combining oscillators, wavetables, modulation, filters, effects, and automation.

### 🔊 Sound Design

Serum 2 can be used to create almost any type of synth sound:

* **Bass** — sub bass, 808s, reese bass, distorted basses
* **Leads** — bright EDM leads, aggressive synths, melodic leads
* **Pads** — wide atmospheric and cinematic textures
* **Plucks** — short, punchy sounds for melodies and chords
* **Keys** — digital, analog-style, and experimental keyboard sounds
* **FX** — risers, impacts, transitions, noise effects, and soundscapes

A good starting point is to build a simple patch and change only one element at a time. Adjust the wavetable first, then shape the sound with envelopes, LFOs, filters, and effects.

## 🌊 Wavetables & Modulation

Wavetables are one of the most important parts of Serum 2. Moving through different waveform positions can completely change the character of a sound.

For more movement, combine wavetable position with:

* LFO modulation
* Envelope modulation
* Macro controls
* Filter cutoff
* Pitch modulation
* Unison and stereo movement
* Distortion and other effects

For example, a slowly moving LFO can turn a static pad into an evolving texture, while fast modulation can create aggressive rhythmic sounds.

### 🎚️ Modulation Workflow

A simple workflow for creating a new patch:

```text
Oscillator
   ↓
Wavetable Position
   ↓
Envelope / LFO
   ↓
Filter
   ↓
Distortion / FX
   ↓
Final Mix
```

Don't add modulation everywhere immediately. A few carefully chosen modulation sources usually produce a cleaner and more controllable patch.

## 🎹 Presets & Sound Libraries

Presets are useful for learning how different sounds are constructed. Instead of only using a preset as-is, open its modulation assignments and effects to understand what makes it work.

Useful preset categories include:

`Bass` · `Leads` · `Pads` · `Plucks` · `Keys` · `FX` · `Arps` · `Experimental`

For your own library, keeping presets organized by **type, genre, or purpose** makes it much easier to find the right sound later.

When downloading third-party presets or wavetables, always check the creator's license before redistributing them.

## ⚙️ Getting Started

The easiest way to learn Serum 2 is to avoid trying to understand every control at once.

Start with:

**1. Oscillators** — learn how the waveform affects the basic tone.

**2. Wavetables** — experiment with different positions and shapes.

**3. Envelopes** — control attack, decay, sustain, and release.

**4. LFOs** — introduce movement and rhythm.

**5. Filters** — remove frequencies and shape the character of the sound.

**6. Effects** — add distortion, delay, reverb, compression, and other processing.

**7. Macros** — combine several parameters into simple performance controls.

Once these fundamentals are familiar, creating your own presets becomes much easier.

## 💡 Quick Sound Design Tips

**For a bigger bass:** keep the sub range controlled and avoid excessive effects in the low frequencies.

**For a wider lead:** experiment with unison, stereo processing, and subtle modulation rather than simply increasing every stereo parameter.

**For atmospheric pads:** use longer envelopes, slow modulation, filtering, and reverb.

**For aggressive sounds:** try wavetable movement combined with distortion, filtering, and fast modulation.

**For cleaner mixes:** don't rely on presets unchanged. Remove unnecessary low frequencies and adjust the patch to fit the rest of the track.
